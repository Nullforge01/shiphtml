Lesson 13: Semantic HTML

Time: 8 min | Goal: Use tags that describe their meaning

Why Semantic > div

<div> means nothing. <nav> means navigation. Screen readers, SEO, and other devs understand semantic tags instantly.

<header> - Intro content, logo, nav
<nav> - Navigation links
<main> - Main page content, only 1 per page
<section> - Thematic grouping of content
<article> - Self-contained content like blog post
<aside> - Sidebar, related content
<footer> - Copyright, links, contact info

Page Structure Example

<body>
  <header>
    <nav>...</nav>
  </header>
  <main>
    <section>
      <article>...</article>
    </section>
    <aside>...</aside>
  </main>
  <footer>...</footer>
</body>

When to Use What

<section> = Chapter of content. Has a heading.

<article> = Standalone. Could be RSS feed item.

<div> = Only for styling. No semantic meaning.

SEO boost: Google ranks semantic HTML higher. <article> tells Google this is the main content. <nav> tells it to ignore links for content ranking.

Your Task

Convert the div soup to semantic HTML:

1. Change outer <div class="header"> to <header>

2. Change <div class="nav"> to <nav>

3. Change <div class="main"> to <main>

4. Change <div class="article"> to <article>

5. Change <div class="footer"> to <footer>

Keep the classes for styling. Click Run - looks identical, but means more.
